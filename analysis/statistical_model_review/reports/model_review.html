2. AADT is a coverage and interpretation issue

exposure = AADT × Length was verified. Keeping raw AADT is Bryan's intentional modeling choice. It preserves coverage; it does not turn team-flagged default values into measured traffic. Screening leaves 63.7% of training segments and 64.8% of test segments without AADT, versus 18.6% and 12.2% with the raw values. Missingness is associated with road types and matched outcomes; complete-case comparisons would change the sample.

traffic_counted exactly equals aadt_clean.notna(). In the EDA copy it is also named aadt_available_screened to describe what was checked. Neither name establishes that the source was a direct traffic count; the supplied AADT_Deriv codes vary within flag=1. The 1,154 default-value screen is the team's assumption and needs confirmation from source metadata. Raw exposure is a proxy with AADT × miles units, not total observed vehicle-miles across the crash window.

3. Why separate length from AADT?

Longer segments offer more opportunity to contain a recorded crash. The alternatives use log1p(Shape__Length) in meters and log1p(AADT) separately, allowing distinct associations. The original source Length is in miles. Surface width, connections, the arterial interaction, road type, sidewalks, and the availability flag remain available. No crash-derived feature, test-block identifier, or image detection was added as a predictor.

4. Training-only candidate selection

Nine predefined candidates: original logistic regression; separate-length/AADT logistic models with C=0.1 or 1, using raw or screened AADT; and shallow histogram gradient boosting with 7 or 15 leaves, again using raw or screened AADT. Four GroupKFold folds used only the 50 training blocks. Imputation, scaling, and encoding were fitted within each training fold. Boosting used 150 iterations, learning rate 0.06, minimum 40 samples per leaf, L2=5, and no random early-stopping split.

Selection used the highest mean fold AP, retaining the original unless the improvement reached 0.005. Fold SD is variability across four areas, not a confidence interval. A timestamped configuration and hashes were saved before evaluating the selected candidate on the existing test set.

This is exploratory evidence. The team previously inspected this test set and used outcomes in upstream feature decisions. We cannot restore a pristine holdout retrospectively. GroupKFold keeps blocks separate but does not buffer adjacent roads, so nearby areas can remain correlated. The paired bootstrap resamples whole test blocks (1,000 draws, seed 506); it cannot account for prior exploration, matching errors, missing imagery, or causal confounding. Top-10% summaries are descriptive and use an arbitrary ordering within tied scores.

6. Recommended team handoff

  • Keep raw AADT, screened AADT, the default-value flag, and AADT derivation metadata separately. Document exposure as a proxy and describe the availability flag accurately.
  • Use the CV-selected variant as an experimental candidate. Before adopting it, agree on a final buffered spatial or later-period evaluation that will not be used repeatedly for model selection.
  • Ask Bryan to confirm the crash observation window and spatial matching sensitivity. The archive supports label agreement checks, but not independent reconstruction of road-to-crash matching.
  • Use road-type and AADT-status subgroup tables to describe coverage and failure modes. Do not select a model from their test scores; small subgroups are unstable.
  • Add Chun's image coverage and detections only after a keyed segment-level handoff; no image benefit is claimed here. Statistical EDA, model validation, and geospatial work should remain attributable.
